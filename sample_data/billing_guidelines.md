# Independent Practice Billing & Claims Guidelines (Sample Reference Document)

This is a synthetic reference document created for testing and demonstration purposes only.
It does not contain real patient data or real payer policy and should not be used for
actual billing decisions.

## Section 1: Common Reasons for Claim Denial

### 1.1 Missing or Invalid Prior Authorization
Claims for procedures that require prior authorization will be denied if the authorization
number is missing, expired, or does not match the billed procedure code. Prior authorization
is typically required for imaging (MRI, CT), certain specialist referrals, and non-emergency
surgical procedures. Practices should verify authorization status before the date of service,
not at the time of billing.

### 1.2 Diagnosis and Procedure Code Mismatch
A claim will be denied if the ICD-10 diagnosis code does not support the medical necessity
of the CPT procedure code billed. For example, billing a knee MRI (CPT 73721) with a
diagnosis code unrelated to musculoskeletal complaints will trigger an automatic denial
under most commercial payer edits.

### 1.3 Duplicate Claim Submission
Submitting the same claim twice, even with minor formatting differences, is one of the
most common denial reasons for small practices. This typically happens when a claim is
resubmitted before the payer's original adjudication cycle (usually 14-30 days) has
completed.

### 1.4 Timely Filing Limits
Most commercial payers require claims to be submitted within 90 to 180 days of the date
of service. Medicaid programs often have shorter windows, sometimes as low as 90 days.
Claims submitted after this window are denied regardless of medical necessity.

### 1.5 Eligibility and Coverage Termination
Claims are denied when the patient's coverage was inactive on the date of service. This
frequently happens when patients change insurance plans mid-month and the front desk does
not re-verify eligibility at each visit.

## Section 2: Recommended Verification Workflow

Practices should verify the following four items before submitting any claim:
1. Active eligibility on the exact date of service (not just at the start of the month)
2. Valid prior authorization number, if applicable, matching the billed procedure
3. Diagnosis code that supports medical necessity for the procedure billed
4. Confirmation that an identical claim has not already been submitted for this
   date of service and procedure combination

## Section 3: Appeal Process for Denied Claims

If a claim is denied, practices generally have 30 to 180 days (payer-dependent) to file
a formal appeal. The appeal should include: the original claim, the denial reason code,
supporting clinical documentation, and a corrected claim if the denial was due to a coding
error. Appeals citing missing prior authorization should include documentation showing the
service was medically urgent, if applicable, as urgent care exceptions may apply.

## Section 4: Prior Authorization Requirements by Service Type

- Advanced imaging (MRI, CT, PET scans): Prior authorization required for most commercial
  and Medicare Advantage plans.
- Physical therapy beyond 12 visits: Many plans require authorization renewal after the
  initial visit allotment.
- Durable medical equipment over $500: Authorization required before the equipment is
  dispensed to the patient.
- Specialist referrals: Required under HMO plans; typically not required under PPO plans.
